Role and Importance of Human Resources

Every dealership tracks its stock, its enquiry funnel and its working capital closely, and reviews them weekly. Manpower rarely gets the same attention, even though it is usually the second or third largest line of cost after inventory. Also, India’s employment law has been rewritten and came into force in November 2025. Because labour is a concurrent subject, the HR must be aware of it.

Why this matters in our ecosystem

Three things make a dealership unusually sensitive to how its people are managed.

First, the product sells through a person. A customer buying a car will spend several hours with one consultant across two or three visits, and will make a decision that they cannot easily reverse. Their confidence in that individual is a large part of the decision. This is not a transaction that survives an indifferent or poorly informed salesperson.

Second, attrition costs far more than it appears to. When a sales consultant with three months of tenure leaves, the dealership loses the recruitment effort, the training investment, the productive time of whoever was mentoring them, and the live enquiries they were carrying. It then pays the same costs again for the replacement, who takes a further two to three months to become productive. A dealership that turns over half its front-line sales team in a year is effectively running with a permanently inexperienced floor, no matter how many people are on the roll.

Third, the relationship outlives the sale. A customer bought from a person, and expects that person to still be there when they come in for their first service or to refer a friend. Every unmanaged exit orphans a set of customers. The ones who feel orphaned are the ones who do not come back.

Culture will drive our operations on ground

"Progress is built on courage, collaboration, and commitment."

Culture is not a poster in the corridor. It is what a new joiner works out in their first fortnight by watching how people behave. In a dealership it is visible in small things: whether the morning meeting is a review or a public dressing-down, whether a consultant who reports a mistake is thanked or shouted at, whether walk-ins are allocated by a rule transparently or by favour.

We would ask dealer partners to aim for a workplace where the following are true.

  • Targets are demanding, and the means of achieving them are not negotiable. Nobody is rewarded for a number reached by cutting a corner with a customer.
  • Problems surface early, because the person who reports one is not punished for it.
  • Enquiries, walk-ins and demonstration vehicles are allocated by a published rule.
  • Managers coach on the floor rather than reviewing from a cabin.
  • Recognition is prompt and specific. Criticism is private.
  • Weekly offs and sanctioned leave are honoured. Long hours are not treated as a substitute for commitment.
  • Nobody is spoken to in an uncomfortable way.

Where these hold, the processes in this manual will work. Where they do not, no process will compensate, and the dealership will spend its time recruiting replacements for people who left for reasons no one wrote down.

If these five are being done, the dealership has a functioning HR practice.

    Functional reporting vs. operational coordination

    Functional reporting (solid line)

    The person this employee works for.

    Operational coordination (dashed line)

    A working relationship the employee must maintain to do the job.

     Functional reportingOperational coordination
    Sets goals and KPIsYesNo, but may be consulted
    Conducts the reviewYesProvides input to the functional manager
    Approves leaveYesNo
    Directs day-to-day workYesOnly within the agreed area of coordination
    Initiates disciplinary actionYesNo, raises it with the functional manager
    1.3

    Workforce Attrition

    Attrition Rate (%) = (Number of Employees Who Left During the Period ÷ Average Number of Employees During the Period) × 100

    Average Headcount = (Beginning Headcount + Ending Headcount) ÷ 2

    Example

    Employees at start of month = 120

    Employees at end of month = 110

    Employees who left during month = 8

    Average Headcount: (120 + 110) ÷ 2 = 115

    Monthly Attrition Rate: (8 ÷ 115) × 100 = 6.96%

    Cost of an Exit

    Total cost of one exit = vacancy loss + pipeline loss + replacement cost + ramp-up loss

    • Vacancy loss = (monthly team retail before the exit - monthly team retail during the vacancy) x months vacant x average gross margin per vehicle
    • Pipeline loss = number of open leads held x cost per lead
    • Replacement cost = sourcing cost + referral or agency payout + induction and training cost
    • Ramp-up loss = shortfall against target during the ramp-up period x average gross margin per vehicle

    CHAPTER 3

    Recruitment & Selection

    "Don't hire for only skills, hire for attitude."
    OBJECTIVE:

    To ensure the dealership has the right people in the right roles through smart recruiting, assessment and onboarding.

    CUSTOMER IMPACT:

    Right people in the right role, and in sufficient quantity enhances the Customer Journey by delivering better experience.

    A standardised hiring process is key to a consistent customer experience across the network. A poor dealership recruitment process leads to:

    • High staff turnover.
    • Increased hiring costs.
    • Increased training cost.
    • Burnout of strong performers while covering for vacant roles.
    • Loss of vehicle retail.
    • Decline in loyalty and repeat business.

    Therefore, this chapter standardises the JSW recruitment and selection process across its dealer network.

    Principles

    • Hire on knowledge, skill and attitude (refer 3A. Candidate Evaluation Sheet).
    • No discrimination or hiring below the age of 18 years.
    • Promote internal mobility before external hiring.
    • Anti-poaching - consult JSW area office before contacting any fellow JSW dealership partner employee.
    • Pay salary on the 5th day of every month.

    Incentive Scheme Best Practices:

    • Balance Bookings and Retails: Incentive schemes should reward both confirmed bookings and retail deliveries. Cancelled booking needs to be taken out in next month.
    • Publish the Incentive Scheme on the First Day of the Month: All signed-off schemes should be released on the first working day of the next month. Mid-month target changes should be avoided.
    • Keep Monthly Schemes Simple: Limit incentive calculations to a maximum of 3-4 performance parameters. Sales consultants should be able to estimate their own earnings without requiring HR or Finance support.
    • Link Incentives to Business Priority: Introduce model-wise boosters for vehicles identified as strategic priorities by JSW Motors. Additional incentives may be offered for EV variants, newly launched products, or slow-moving inventory.
    • Pay Incentives Along with Salary: Incentives should always be processed as part of the monthly salary cycle. Delayed payouts reduce the motivational impact of the scheme.
    • Protect New Joiners and New Dealerships: New sales consultants should receive a realistic ramp-up target for the first 60-90 days. New dealerships may operate under a simplified incentive structure until market demand stabilizes.

    Training Management System

    The Training Management System will be a comprehensive, paperless training management platform designed to manage the complete training lifecycle for all relevant role holders across Sales and Service. Pre-training and post-training assessments are also conducted digitally through the Training Management System.

Attendance sheet will be automatically shared with the concerned trainer through email immediately upon training completion.

    The system will also provides dealers with real-time visibility into the training status of their manpower, including nominations, approvals, attendance, training completion, and assessment results. Comprehensive reports will enable stakeholders to monitor training progress and identify pending actions without relying on manual follow-ups, spreadsheets, or email-based communication.

    By digitizing the complete training lifecycle, the Training Management System will ensure greater transparency, faster execution, real-time visibility, improved data accuracy, and significantly reduced administrative effort, while creating a single source of truth for training-related information across the JSW Motors and dealer network.

    Learning Management System (LMS)

    6. Performance Management
    “The key to successful performance management is to focus on the future, not the past. It’s about enabling and empowering employees, not just evaluating them.”
    OBJECTIVE:

    To review employee’s contribution fairly and consistently against agreed goals and good performance to be recognised, gaps to be addressed before they affect the business.

    CUSTOMER IMPACT:

    Regular, fair performance reviews keep employees focused on their job responsibilities and KPIs and ultimately impact the customer experience.

    Performance management improves dealership workforce productivity and satisfaction by aligning individual goals with dealership targets through ongoing feedback. Before diving in, let’s look at some benefits of performance management:

    Benefits for Employers

    • It connects daily work by staff to the larger strategic aims of the dealership.
    • It reduces wasted effort because managers and employee agree on expected outcomes.
    • It allows usage of data to handle promotions, increments, skill-gaps, training and underperformance.
    • It catches performance dips early through regular feedback.

    Benefits for Employees

    • It gives employees a precise understanding of their duties, targets, and how success is measured.
    • It supports professional development, upskilling, and pathways to promotions.
    • It acknowledges good work and rewards high performance, which boosts daily engagement.
    • It replaces surprise reviews with open regular communication, making the daily work environment transparent and stress-free.

    Principles

    • Follow the financial year for performance review cycle.
    • Set measurable SMART goals against the target KPIs.
    • Feedbacks are provided frequently, not saved for only the performance review day.

    6.6 Handling underperformance

    It is important to initiate coaching if the quarterly reviews indicate a consistent decline in performance.

    Step 1: State the Issue Directly

    "Thank you for sitting down with me. I wanted to review your sales target over the last 90 days. Right now, your average units sold is at 1 per month, while your KPI is 4 units per month."

    Step 2: Identify the Root Cause

    • Skill: Don't know how to overcome common objections, close the deal, or demo products properly.
    • Activity: Not enough lead follow ups, not quick on finance or trade-in documentation follow-up.
    • Attitude: Burnout from long hours, low morale, or personal stress.

    Step 3: Co-Create a Corrective Action Plan

    Do not just say "sell more cars" or "pick up the pace." Break down into daily, manageable activities like:

    • Scheduling mandatory roleplay sessions
    • Setting a baseline of 5 logged CRM outbound calls per day
    • Personal mentorship via real-time on the floor observation and feedback

    Step 4: Establish a Clear Timeline & Follow-Up

    "We are going to review your CRM activity every Friday morning at 9:00 AM. My goal is to see you hit your daily activity targets, so your retail number naturally bounces back."

    10. Discipline and Conduct
    "Talent might make you good but it’s your attitude and work ethic that will make you great."
    OBJECTIVE:

    The professional image of JSW motors is directly linked to the way each individual present themselves and behave. Set out clear standards of workplace behaviour and a fair process for addressing misconduct, so issues are handled consistently and employees know what is expected of them.

    CUSTOMER IMPACT:

    A disciplined dealership environment encourages professionalism in every customer interaction. Customer gets fair treatment, timely responses and reliable service leading to trust in JSW brand.

    Principles

    • Be honest, transparent and ethical in every decision, transaction and interaction
    • Respect people & diversity
    • Be accountable for your actions
    • Protect trust and confidentiality
    • Promote a safe and compliant workplace

    Every organisation has their own DNA and ways of doing things. At JSW, (OEM and franchise partners included) we are governed by our pillars of foundation. They include our vision, our behaviours to execute that vision and work ethics. All of this together brings out the essence of working at JSW and supporting our customers.

    Our code of conduct and expected behavioural etiquettes are framed based on the above. These principles as explained below not only define how we work but will take us far into our individual journey as a human being.

    10.1 Code of conduct

    a. Purpose & Scope The following code of Conduct applies to all the retail partner and its employees and sets out the ethical and behavioural requirement that are mandatory. The Code of Conduct is a necessary component to maintain the integrity of JSW brand and guarantee that customers continue to trust us.

    b. Ethical Business & Sales Practices

    Transparency: Be honest about prices, vehicle specs, financing terms, and warranties. There shall be no Hidden fees, deceptive payment, and misleading advertising.

    Allied Business Transactions: Do only authorized accessories fitment, merchandise sales. Give correct written estimates before further fitment and delivery.

    Data Privacy: Ensure that customer personal and financial data is protected. Process credit applications, ID and contact information in compliance with local laws on privacy.

    c. Professional and Behavioural Traits

    Customer First: Treat every visitor with utmost courtesy, responsiveness, and professionalism, irrespective of their buying intentions or abilities.

    Brand Representation: Maintain showroom and staff appearance consistent with JSW brand visual identity guidelines.

    d. Equal Opportunity & Fair Workplace

    Zero Tolerance for Discrimination: Promote diversity and inclusion by providing equal opportunity in hiring, promotion, and daily operations without regard to race, gender, religion, age, sexual orientation, or disability.

    Safe Environment: Maintain a workplace free from harassment (including sexual harassment), bullying, and intimidation.

    Fair Compensation: Comply with all local labour laws, ensuring fair & timely wages and safe working conditions for all dealership staff.

    e. Anti-Corruption & Compliance

    Bribery & Kickbacks: Do not offer, give, solicit, or accept bribes, kickbacks, or inappropriate gifts from customers, suppliers, lenders, or government officials.

    Conflicts of Interest: Disclose any personal or financial relationships that might influence business decisions or give an unfair business advantage.

    Legal Adherence: Comply fully with all local automotive, consumer protection, environmental, and financial regulations.

    f. Reporting & Enforcement

    Duty to Report: Employees must promptly report any suspected violations of this Code to the HR Manager or through the organisation’s anonymous compliance policy/channel.

    Protection for Whistleblowers: Employees who raise concerns honestly and responsibly will not face any form of retaliation or discrimination.

    Consequences: Violations will result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.

    The basics we expect from everyone

    Be punctual and come to work in the required uniform

    Be honest with customers about price, product and delivery date.

    Follow the correct process when handling customer payments and documents.

    Keep customer information safe and confidential.

    Do not accept money, gifts or favours from financiers, insurers or vendors.

    Treat customers and colleagues with courtesy and respect

    Ensure all work and records are entered accurately in the system.

    Follow safety rules & procedures, including during test drives.

    • Every employee receives a copy of the Code of Conduct when they join the dealership and is expected to understand and follow its requirements.

    Types of Misconduct and Their Examples

    Misconduct is divided into two kinds:

    Minor misconduct

    Minor misconduct is usually a mistake, lapse in discipline. It is normally handled through a conversation with manager

    Examples
    Coming late repeatedly, or leaving early without permission
    Absent without informing anybody
    Not wearing uniform, or poor grooming
    Untidy work area, or not following the display standard
    Not attending training after being nominated
    Small arguments with colleagues

    Major misconduct

    It refers to serious actions that involves dishonesty, safety violations or behaviours that can harm people, the dealership environment, customers and JSW brand. Such cases are investigated through formal disciplinary process and may lead to termination of employment. please refer Section 10.2 for the same.

    Examples
    Theft or dishonesty with cash, stock, accessories or fuel
    Taking a customer’s payment into a personal account
    Taking money, commission or a gift from a financier, insurer, broker or vendor
    Booking a retail that has not happened or misrepresentation of any record
    Forging a signature or a document, including on a customer’s behalf
    Sharing or selling customer data
    Giving false information at the time of hiring, such as a qualification or a previous salary
    Sexual harassment (see 10.3)
    Abusive language, threats, or physical violence
    Coming to work under the influence of alcohol or drugs
    Driving a dealership or customer vehicle without a valid licence, or driving dangerously
    Deliberately damaging dealership or customer property
    Being absent without information for a long period
    Doing a job for another employer while employed here, dual employment
    Deliberately not following a lawful instruction from a manager
    Any act that breaks the law and impacting JSW images

    These lists are examples, not the complete list. Something not written here can still be misconduct.

    Employee salaries cannot be reduced as a punishment unless permitted by law. If the dealership needs to recover any loss, it must follow the proper legal process.

    The enquiry

    If an employee does not attend the enquiry despite receiving proper notice, the enquiry may proceed in their absence. All notices issued and records of non-attendance should be documented and retained.

    Suspension during an enquiry

    • Used only where the person's presence would affect the enquiry, or where the matter is serious, such as theft or violence.
    • It is not a punishment. It is a pause while the facts are checked.
    • A subsistence allowance is paid during suspension, as the law requires.
    • If the person is cleared, they return to the same job and the balance salary is paid.

    10.3 POSH: Prevention of sexual harassment

    This is governed by the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013.

    What counts as sexual harassment

    Any unwelcome behaviour of a sexual nature. This includes:

    • Physical contact or advances.
    • Asking for or demanding sexual favours.
    • Sexually coloured remarks, jokes or comments about a person's body.
    • Showing pornography.
    • Any other unwelcome physical, verbal or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature, including messages, photographs and social media.

    The workplace includes the showroom, the workshop, the stockyard, the parking area, a customer's home during a visit, a test drive, an event, an outing and a work trip. It also covers work-related messages and calls outside office hours.

    The Internal Committee

    Every dealership with ten or more employees must have an Internal Committee also called as IC. This is a legal requirement, not a recommendation.

    CompositionAt least half of the members must be women
    TermThree years

    • No action of any kind may be taken against the woman for having complained. Any pressure or unfair treatment after a complaint is major misconduct.

    What the dealership must do

    • Display the names and contact details of the IC members where everybody can see them.
    • Explain POSH to every new joiner during induction, including men.
    • Run an awareness session at least once a year through certified POSH trainer.
    • Train the IC members through certified POSH trainer.

    • Include a POSH section in every appointment letter and the conduct code.

    10.4 Grievance handling

    A grievance is any problem an employee has at work: pay, leave, a manager, workload, unfair treatment, facilities, or anything else.

The committee should also include adequate representation of women. HR is responsible for monitoring legal requirements and ensuring the committee is formed in a timely manner.

    • Be punctual, professional and customer focused at all times
    • Follow company processes and legal requirements without shortcuts
    • Report concerns or misconduct promptly and responsibly.
    • Treat customers, colleagues, and business partners with respect.
    • Protect the reputation of the dealership and the JSW brand through ethical conduct
    • Nobody is punished for raising a complaint or a grievance in good faith.

    If you cannot come to work

    • Inform the reporting manager before the shift starts.
    • Then apply for leave in the system on the same day or the next working day.
    • Absent without informing your manager is treated as leave without pay, and repeated cases are a disciplinary matter.
    • Absent for a period more than 5 working days without information is absconding which may lead to legal proceedings.

    Attendance records

    • Leave record must be updated.
    • HR closes attendance for the month by the date fixed for payroll, usually the 25th.
    • Any correction must be requested before the closing date. After payroll is processed, it is adjusted the subsequent month.
    • Records are kept as the law requires, and may be kept in electronic form.

    • Anybody driving on dealership work must hold a valid licence. Traffic fines from careless driving are the employee’s own liability.
    • Settle claims quickly.

    11.6 Best Practices

    • Working hours, week off and the holiday list are displayed at every outlet.
    • The roster for the month is published before the month begins.
    • Everybody marks their own attendance. Marking for somebody else is a disciplinary matter.
    • Leave is applied for in the system and approved in writing. Verbal permission is not leave.
    • A leave application is answered within two working days.
    • Working on a week off earns a compensatory off within 30 days, or overtime pay as per law.
    • Women working after dark do so only with consent and with safe transport arranged, and they should not be working alone.
    • Travel claims are submitted within 7 days and settled with the next salary.
    • Where the law in the state gives more than this chapter, the law applies.
